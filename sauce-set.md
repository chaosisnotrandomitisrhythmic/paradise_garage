# Sauce — A Salsa Set

Forty tracks, 3 h 13 min, drawn from Ana Cuna's *Sauce* playlist (267 of its tracks are
in the library, recorded 5–7 Nov 2026). The arc starts with Cuban son, builds through 1970s
Fania New York salsa to two peaks, takes a breather on salsa romántica, then goes to a
boogaloo and Latin soul room where Paradise Garage technique works, and ends in the small hours.
M3U: [`playlists/sauce-salsa-set.m3u8`](playlists/sauce-salsa-set.m3u8), also at `~/Music/Playlists/` on xenolaptop.

## What the analysis says about mixing salsa

All 267 tracks were re-measured for this page (librosa beat tracking, a tempo fit every
30 s, intro bass level, ending shape). Three findings shape how to DJ them:

1. **The BPMs in the catalog are half-time.** Ingest put most salsa at 90–110 BPM. On the
   quarter note it sits at **172–216**, with most tracks between 180 and 200. Boogaloo and Latin
   soul really are around 110–136. Use the numbers on this page, or halve/double in Mixxx.
2. **Most of these records drift.** Live bands, no click track: the median track moves 1–3 BPM
   from one half-minute to the next, and the hot ones accelerate into the mambo. *María Lionza*
   goes 181 → 195, *Juan Pachanga* 210 → 226, *Pastime Paradise* 207 → 221. A Mixxx sync lock
   on these follows a grid that doesn't exist. Only about 50 tracks are steady
   (spread under 1 BPM): the El Remolón cuts, the remixes (*Fried Neck Bones*, *Virgencita*),
   *Idilio*, *Amigo*, *Tromboranga*, *Qué Manera de Quererte*, *La Marea*, *Arranca en Fa*.
3. **213 of the 266 fade out, 46 end on a hard stop.** Salsa endings are arranged, often
   with a final *¡pa!* hit.

### So: blend or not?

Salsa dancers dance the arrangement: intro, verse, *coro-pregón* montuno, mambo, ending. A
social floor wants the song played whole, so the default transition is **short and on the
one**:

- **Hard stop** → start the next track on the last hit, or one beat after it. No gap over
  a second; the cut is the transition.
- **Fade** → overlap the last 4–8 bars of the fade with the new intro. Nudge tempo by hand
  (pitch fader, no sync), close enough for 15 seconds.
- **Bass-light intro** (piano, percussion, horns before the tumbao enters) → this is where
  you can layer. Bring it in under the outgoing montuno and swap the low end on the downbeat
  where the new bass enters. In this set: *Micaela* (bass 0.0× the body level for the first
  12 s), *Call My Name* (0.0×), *Basta Ya* (0.2×), *Charachacha* (0.1×), *El Día de Suerte*,
  *Sonido Bestial*, *Mosaico Boogaloo*, Adalberto Santiago's *Quítate la Máscara*.

**The Paradise Garage bass swap carries over, with two salsa rules:**

- **Phrase on the clave, not on 16 bars.** One clave cycle is two bars. If one song is in
  3-2 and the other in 2-3, a bar-aligned blend puts the two claves against each other and
  the floor feels it even if the BPM matches. Shift the incoming track by one bar.
- **The tumbao anticipates.** Salsa bass plays the *and* of 2 and beat 4, not the one.
  Two tumbaos on top of each other blur fast, so swap quickly (one or two bars), don't
  ride both basses for eight.

Where long blends work: the **Garage room** (section VII). Boogaloo and Latin soul have a
drummer on a steady backbeat and a repeating vamp, the tempos ladder from 110 to 136, and
the keys stay around 9B. That's home ground for the Xone:96 filters.

EQ: these are old masters, mid-heavy, often mono. Don't cut the mids hard on a swap; the
piano montuno and the horns live there. ReplayGain tags are on every file (−18 LUFS
reference); turn on ReplayGain in Mixxx, the masters range from −15 to −8 LUFS.

  Que Va Caer* (183, 10B), *Arranca en Fa* (236 → 118 feel, 4B, bassless intro).

  *La Mucura* (215), *Descarga Caliente* (219), *Merecumbe* (214).
