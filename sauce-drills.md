# Sauce — Mixing Drills

Fifteen drills, 30 tracks, 2 h 36 min. The tracks come in pairs (a few in threes), in deck order:
the first track goes on deck A, the next on deck B. Each drill practises one way of getting from
one record to the next, from the salsa-floor basics to the Paradise Garage moves, and ends at
the door into house. Background and measurements: [Sauce — A Salsa Set](sauce-set).

M3U: [`playlists/sauce-mixing-drills.m3u8`](playlists/sauce-mixing-drills.m3u8), also at `~/Music/Playlists/` on xenolaptop. Load it into a Mixxx playlist and
work through it top to bottom, one drill per sitting if you like.

BPM is quarter-note (start → end of the track); *steady* means the tempo moves less than
1 BPM through the song. Turn Mixxx sync **off** for drills 1–8, **on** for 9–11 and 15.

## Part 1 · The salsa floor

**1 · Cut on the hard stop.** Willie Colón *Juana Peña* (198→200, 7B, ends on a hit) → Willie
Colón & Héctor Lavoe *Ché Ché Colé* (204, 8B).
Cue *Ché Ché Colé* on its first downbeat. Hit play on *Juana Peña*'s final hit, not after it.
Listen for: no gap, no flam. Do it ten times; it's the transition you'll use most.

**2 · Let the cut hide a tempo change.** Oscar D'León *Lloraras* (185→189, 1A, hard stop) →
El Gran Combo *La Fiesta de Pilito* (199, 8A).
Fifteen BPM and a key change apart, which no blend could cover. On a hard stop, the ear resets.
Listen for: the floor feels a lift, not a jolt.

**3 · Short overlap on the fade, matched drift.** Héctor Lavoe *Periódico de Ayer* (198→201,
5A) → Grupo Niche *Cali Pachanguero* (198→202, 5A).
Same key, and both speed up the same way. Set B to A's tempo *at the end of A*, not the
start. Bring B in over the last 8 bars of the fade.

**4 · Ride the pitch by hand.** Willie Colón & Héctor Lavoe *Calle Luna Calle Sol* (179→183,
5A) → El Gran Combo *Aguacero* (182, 5A).
A drifts while B is steady. Keep the overlap going by nudging the jog wheel or the pitch
fader while both play. Listen for: the cowbells drifting apart; that's what you correct.

**5 · Layer a bassless intro.** Grupo Niche *Una Aventura* (184→186, 8B) → Sonora Carruseles
*Micaela* (181, 8B, steady; no bass for the first 12 s).
Pitch *Micaela* up to about 185. Start it under *Una Aventura*'s last montuno with B's low EQ
already open (there's nothing there yet). When *Micaela*'s bass enters, kill A's low end on
that downbeat and fade A out.

**6 · Same move, faster and longer.** El Gran Combo *No Hay Cama Pa' Tanta Gente* (213→216,
8B) → Adalberto Santiago *Quítate la Máscara* (216, 8B; bassless intro; 10:48).
The tempo is almost identical, so you can let the layer run for 16 bars. Then let
Adalberto's ten minutes play out.

**7 · Clave check on two steady grids.** Ismael Miranda *Amigo* (185, steady, 6A) → Willie
Colón *Idilio* (188, steady, 7B).
Both hold their tempo, so beatmatch properly and run them together for 8 bars. Then
listen to the clave: if the two clash, shift B by **one bar** (4 beats), not a beat. That's
3-2 against 2-3. Find which way each song goes.

**8 · Two rhythm families.** Ismael Rivera *El Nazareno* (180, 5B) → El Remolón *Liga de Sabor
Tropical* (180, steady, 5A; digital cumbia).
Same tempo, relative keys, but cumbia has no clave and puts its weight on the backbeat. Try
a long blend, then a hard bass swap, and hear which one holds.

## Part 2 · The Garage room

**9 · Double-time to half-time.** Rubén Blades & Pete Rodríguez *Descarga Caliente* (219→215,
9B) → Willie Bobo & Dan The Automator *Fried Neck Bones and Some Home Fries* (110, dead
steady, 9B).
B is exactly half of A, in the same key. Beatmatch one of B's beats to two of A's. When
B comes in the floor feels the pulse halve, the classic way out of salsa into a funk or
house tempo.

**10 · Long blend with filters.** → Willie Bobo *Funky Sneakers* (109→111, 9A).
9B to 9A, same tempo. Run both for 32 bars: high-pass A slowly while low-passing B open.
Pure Xone:96.

**11 · Bass swap on the one.** → Joe Bataan *Call My Name* (108→110, 9B; no bass for 12 s).
The Paradise Garage swap: B's lows down, both mids and highs up, then trade the low end in
one move on a downbeat where B's bass enters. Swap fast, in one or two bars.

**12 · Pitch ladder.** Pete & Louie *Evil Ways* (116→114, 9B) → Mongo Santamaría *Sofrito*
(118→116, 7B) → Mongo Santamaría *Watermelon Man* (120→119, 7B).
Each blend raises the tempo by 2–3 BPM. Push the pitch fader up a little during each
overlap so the room climbs without noticing.

**13 · Loop the break, drop the next.** Fania All Stars *Descarga Fania* (163→162, 8B, 9:19) →
Willie Colón & Rubén Blades *Siembra* (166→162, 8B).
Find a percussion-only stretch in the descarga, loop 4 or 8 bars, and drop *Siembra*'s intro
over the loop. Same tempo and key. Let the loop out once *Siembra*'s horns land.

**14 · Echo out, new world.** Rubén Blades *Juan Pachanga* (accelerates 210 → 226, hard stop) →
Eddie Palmieri *Azúcar* (~150, 7A).
No matching possible. Throw an echo or reverb on *Juan Pachanga*'s last hit, let it ring
out, and start Palmieri's piano in the tail. The space between them is the transition.

**15 · The door into house.** Celia Cruz & Johnny Pacheco *Virgencita (Happy Colors Remix)*
(115, steady, 8A) → Liquid Liquid *Cavern* (115, 8A) → Incredible Bongo Band *Apache*
(118, 8A).
Three records, one key, one tempo, and congas or bongos all the way through. Sync on.
From *Apache* the house crates are open: *Can You Feel It*, *French Kiss*, *Rich in Paradise*
(the Bass Swap Drills) sit at 112–123 and 8A–9B.
